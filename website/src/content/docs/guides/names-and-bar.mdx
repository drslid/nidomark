## Quick access

The new bookmarks bar opens with the icons of the sites you open most, such as your mailbox, your video site or your home dashboard. Their names are hidden: the icon is enough to recognise them, and a dozen fit in the space of three names.

- **How they are chosen.** With the history allowed (**Put the most visited sites first**, on by default), Nidomark counts the visits of each site, typed visits counting twice. Each site gets one icon, its shortest address: the home page rather than an article. Without the history, the shortcuts already in your bar keep their place.
- **How many.** Choose none, 5, 10 (the default) or 20 on the start screen or in the settings.
- **What never goes there.** Adult content, sites that no longer answer and duplicates.
- **Bookmarklets** and browser pages kept in your bar stay in it, with their names.
- Quick access only changes the bookmarks bar when the bar itself is sorted.

Quick access is a folder like the others in the review: drag a bookmark onto it to add it, or move one out with its menu.

## Folder names

Choose a style on the start screen, in the top bar of the review, or in the settings:

| Style                | A top-level folder in the bar | A subfolder  | One of your projects |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------ | -------------------- |
| Emoji only (default) | 💻                            | 📡 Tech news | Wedding 2025         |
| Emoji + name         | 💻 Tech                       | 📡 Tech news | Wedding 2025         |
| Name only            | Tech                          | Tech news    | Wedding 2025         |

A row of emoji keeps the bookmarks bar short: a dozen folders fit where three names would. Subfolders keep their names, because a list of emoji is hard to read once a folder is open, and so does every folder when the new tree goes to Other bookmarks.

Your names win. A folder of yours that Nidomark recognises lends its name to the category: if you called it "Recipes", the new folder is "🍳 Recipes", not "Cooking". A folder of yours that holds a project is recreated under its own name. The names you give in the review are kept for the next sorts.

Change the emoji of a folder in the review: open it and click the tile next to its name. The picker offers common emoji, any emoji you type, or none. To give one folder its own style, open it and choose under **Display**: a project folder can keep its name in a bar of emoji, for instance.

Folder names follow the language of your browser. Nidomark speaks 21 languages and picks Chrome's own: English, French, Spanish, Italian, German, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Dutch, Turkish, Arabic, Hindi, Bengali, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese (simplified and traditional). Your own folders are recognised in any of them: a folder named "Rezepte" or "Рецепты" lends its name to the recipes.

## Cleaner titles

Nidomark proposes cleaner titles for the bookmarks it recreates: notification counters such as "(3)" and repeated site names go, and long titles in quick access are shortened. The move menu of a bookmark switches between the cleaner title and the original one.

## Bookmarks shown as icons only

With **Bookmarks bar as icons**, on by default, the quick access bookmarks keep only their icon. The strip at the top of the review previews the new bar: click a bookmark to show or hide its name, or use **Icon only** in its menu.

Only the bookmarks right in the bar can show their icon alone. Chrome always shows a bookmark's icon, and a bookmark without a name inside a folder shows its address instead, so the choice is offered in the bar only. Folders, on the other hand, can show an emoji, an emoji and a name, or a name.

Nidomark keeps the original names, even across sorts. To show them all again later, open **Settings**, then **Icon-only bookmarks** → **Show all names again**.
