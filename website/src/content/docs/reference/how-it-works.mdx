---
title: How sorting works
description: The evidence Nidomark uses for each bookmark, how the local model decides, and how the new tree is built.
---

Nidomark sorts in three passes: it decides what each bookmark is, reads the pages it needs, then builds a new tree around your habits. Applying it recreates every bookmark in that tree and moves your previous tree, untouched, into a dated backup.

## 1. What each bookmark is

The decision follows the most certain evidence first.

| Order | Evidence                                                                 | Result                                   |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1     | A private address, a `.local` or `.lan` name, a dynamic DNS or remote-access name, or several services on one personal domain | 🖥️ Home services |
| 2     | The site no longer answers (when dead links are checked)                 | 🚫 Unreachable sites                     |
| 3     | Adult content: its site, its address, an explicit title, or a page that declares it | 🔞 Porn                       |
| 4     | Your own choice in an earlier sort, for this address or this site        | The category you chose                   |
| 5     | A known site, such as `netflix.com` or the home page of `korben.info`   | Its category                             |
| 6     | A typical address, such as `.gouv.fr`, a regional bank or a school portal | Its category                            |
| 7     | A guide about an application you host, such as Jellyfin or Home Assistant | Self-hosting or Home automation         |
| 8     | The local model                                                          | The category it clearly prefers          |
| 9     | The folder you had put it in: its name ("Recipes", "Zigbee"), or the subject most of its other bookmarks share | Settles a close call, or files a bookmark that says nothing by itself |
| 10    | At least two bookmarks of the same site already filed in one category    | That category                            |

**The subject comes first, so that each subject has one place.** A video, a course, a forum thread, a news article or a product about cooking goes to Cooking, about home automation to Home automation. Videos, Courses & tutorials, Reference, Shopping, Social networks and Tech news keep what has no subject of its own.

Sites that publish on every subject are never filed by their address alone. On YouTube, GitHub, Reddit or Wikipedia, and past the home page of a store (Amazon), a tech news site (Korben, Numerama) or a course platform (OpenClassrooms), the subject of the page decides; the site's own category, such as Videos for YouTube, applies only when the page names no subject. The site's name in a title ("… - YouTube") is not taken for a subject, and these sites never take part in step 10.

**Your folders say what their bookmarks are about.** A bookmark in your "Recipes" folder leans to Cooking; one in a folder whose other bookmarks are mostly about home automation leans to Home automation. Clear evidence still wins (an adult site leaves any folder), a folder of a format such as "Tutorials" or "YouTube videos" pulls less than a folder of a subject, and folders without a subject ("Misc", "Read later") say nothing.

## 2. The local model

Nidomark runs [multilingual-e5-small](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/multilingual-e5-small), a small multilingual model, inside the browser with ONNX Runtime. It turns a text into a list of numbers, so that texts about the same thing get close numbers.

- The text of a bookmark is its title, its address and the words of its path, or the real page title from your history when the title is vague and history is on.
- The model compares it with about 300 typical bookmarks written by hand, a few for each category, and with the bookmarks of your collection already recognised in step 1, which teach it your own vocabulary. A page of a platform is not compared with the examples of the same platform: "youtube.com" would weigh more than its subject.
- The five closest examples share a vote; the description of each category, the keywords found in the title and in the words of the address (`recettes-faciles.fr` speaks of recipes), and your folders (step 9) add to it.
- A category is chosen only when it clearly leads the second one. Otherwise the bookmark is unclear, and the three categories it leans to most become its suggestions.

## 3. Reading the pages

When reading sites is allowed, the page of each unclear bookmark is read and scored the same way, the page and the title together. A page that says almost nothing, such as a login form, is completed with the home page of its site. A page still unclear after that follows the other bookmarks of its site when at least two of them agree (an unclear post of a tech blog goes with its other posts). The rest go to ❓ To check, with the reason and up to three suggestions. See [Sites, dead links and duplicates](/guides/sites-and-duplicates/).

## 4. Building the new tree

Every bookmark gets a place: nothing stays where it was.

**Quick access opens the bookmarks bar.** The sites you visit most, one icon per site, its shortest address, counted from your history on this computer; without the history, the shortcuts already in your bar. Bookmarklets of the bar stay there. Adult content, dead sites and duplicates never do. See [Quick access and folder names](/guides/names-and-bar/).

**Your folders guide the new ones.** Nidomark reads their names, in any of its 21 languages:

- a folder named after a category or a group, such as "Recipes", "Séries" or "Leisure", lends its name to that category: the new folder is called the way you called it. A name of its subject before its format counts: "Home automation purchases" is about home automation. A folder named after one site or one product ("Netflix", "Linux") tells what it holds but does not rename the whole category;
- a catch-all folder ("Misc", "To sort", "New folder"), a reading list ("Read later") and the root folders of another browser imported as folders ("Imported from Firefox", "Bookmarks Menu", "Bookmarks Toolbar") are emptied: each bookmark joins its subject;
- any other folder whose bookmarks belong together, such as "Wedding 2025" with a venue, a caterer and a dress shop, or "Docker" inside your "Tech" folder, is recreated with its subfolders: under the category or the group that holds most of it, or in 🗂️ My folders when it mixes subjects;
- a folder of a single bookmark is not worth recreating: that bookmark is filed like the others.

Adult content and dead links leave even your own folders.

**New folders are few, three levels at most.**

- About a dozen groups at the top, the most used first: by the visits of their sites when the history is known, else by size.
- A category gets its own subfolder from two bookmarks; Streaming, Porn and Unreachable sites always get their own folder. When a group holds only one category, the folder takes the category's name, for example 📺 Streaming rather than 🎬 Leisure. A category named like its group fills the group folder itself: ✈️ Travel holds the trips, with 🗺️ Maps & transit inside, never "Travel › Travel".
- A category of ten bookmarks or more is split once more, by site ("Netflix", "Marmiton") or by a word shared by its titles ("Zigbee"), from four bookmarks, six subfolders at most. A word and its plural make one subfolder; a platform that publishes on every subject, such as YouTube or GitHub, never makes one, or it would come back under every category.
- No folder appears twice under the same parent, nor inside a folder of the same name: such folders are merged, your own first.
- The top folders go to the destination you chose. Adult content, dead links, ❓ To check and 🧩 Browser links (bookmarklets, browser pages, files) always go to Other bookmarks.

The full list is in [Categories](/reference/categories/).

**A sort of part of your bookmarks adds to the folders already there.** When you choose only some folders, for instance ❓ To check or the bookmarks added since the last sort, the folders an earlier sort made outside them receive what belongs to them, under their own name and at their own place: no second 💻 Tech. A category they lack becomes a new subfolder of its group.

## 5. Applying

Applying creates the new folders, recreates every bookmark in its folder (quick access first, so that its icons come first in the bar), then moves the previous tree, item by item and in order, into "Nidomark backup – _date_" in Other bookmarks. Each change is written to a journal before it happens. See [Backup and undo](/guides/backup-and-undo/).

## 6. Learning from your corrections

During the review, **Analyze again** sorts a folder once more with what you just taught: a bookmark you filed in a folder named after a category teaches it that category, for its address and its site. It reads the pages it had not read yet and never moves the bookmarks you placed yourself.

When you file a bookmark elsewhere than proposed and apply, Nidomark remembers the category you chose, on your computer: for that address, and for its site unless the site publishes on every subject (YouTube, GitHub, Medium…). At the next sort they go there. The names you give to folders are kept too. Undoing a sort forgets what it taught.

A second sort of a tree Nidomark built proposes the same tree: its folders are recognised, and only what changed moves.
